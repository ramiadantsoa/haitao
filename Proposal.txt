Proposal

Overview:
A majority researcher in bioligical sciences are handicaped by a lack of quantitative skills.
Using mathematics, statistics, and programming in their data analyses seems to contradict with their passion for going to the field and study their favorite organisms.
Yet, this is crucial part of the scientific research and they often realize the daunting task late in their career.
This problem is global but is worse in Madagascar where the passion for the organism inversely related with the will and accessibility of learning the skills and analysing the data.
To address this issue, I propose to write a handbook to help Malagasy scientists in the their quantitative skills.
The handbook aims to overcome barriers to learning:

First, the handbook will be free available from PDF and html online.

Second, it will be written in Malagasy although an English version will also be published.

Third, it is focused on active and independant learning.
To achieve that: (a) chapters are short (2 pages) and includes the following sections: intuition behind the concept, rigourous definition (which could be skipped by the reader), examples and R code, and tips and warnings, and link to other sources if thirsty for more.
(b) This is a comprehensive handbook and self-contained: all the topics which will be cross-referenced, no need to look elsewhere.
As of this writing, there are 214 chapters divided into nine parts: basic math, basic stat, basic programming, data wrangling (in base R and tidyverse), data visualization (in base R and tidyverse), community ecology, inferential statistics, intermediate math and stat, and mechanistic modeling.

Proposal:
Doing scientific research could be a fullfilling and rewarding career.
However, many who aspires to become one is often faced with the need to master an unexpected skills and steps until publishing a paper during graduate.
For instance, for a graduate student in ecology, on top of collecting data about their favorite organisms, the student needs to read and digest a bunch of papers, learn to do scientific writing, and effectic scientific communication, and statistics.
In more recent years where the rise of programming like R, it created an additional tools.
For non-native, learning English language is another stuff.
In many cases, these "soft" skills are not necessarily part of the curriculum (see numerous papers on writing, reading, communication and navigating graduate schools).
The degree to which depends these "knowledge gap" are addressed on institution, department, or the specific lab.
Addresssed in a sporadic way.
Bigger problems in lmic.
This could preclude talents from reaching potential and make impacts.

Of all those listed above, the duo programming-statistics is one of the most daunting challenges for scientist with data from the nature.
Statistics have always been feared.
Although taught in the curricula, it is often seen as mandatory.
Often taught by statisticians, and many mathphobia that class, realize later.
The situtation is aggravated by the fact that shift of using R.
R is free so everyone can use it, but the cost that is that it is a programming language.
So unlike other programs like SPSS or STATA, these non-spreadsheet program requires coding and not drop down selection.
A program that could alleviate such burden is likely to transform (people with quantative skills have an edge?), and better paper, better scientists, and good for society.

In Madagascar, one of the most aspiring career is ecology etc.
With species like lemurs, chameleons, or orchids present nowhere else, its biodiversity is a wonder begs for discovery and exploration.
With threats, conservation is also central.
Whereas there is a natural talent in those field, it is counterbalanced by the challenges of doing research.
Even funded research struggle.
Strange paradox between being in the field and learning computer programs and statistics.
Many realises the importance, and there is a huge demand for training.
I have been organizing workshops since, and is always full.
Sporadic.
Unfortunately, the demand is too high, and something needs to be done for wider reach and put students at the same level.

This handbook offers an radical approach to learning quantitative skills for ecologist, bioligist and those with real data.
It is supposed to help in four ways:

First, it will be free as a PDF and HTML and can be downloaded online.

Second, everything will be in one place, cross-referenced.
It will compile most used stats and concepts.
Add a section on basic mathematics to alleviate mathphobia.

Third, the format to what people want to know.
Focuses on inutition so concept are explained in layman's term, strong emphasis on intuition.
All chapters is structured in the same way: a section on intuitive concept, a rigourous definition, an example with R code, and tips and warnings and links to further materials if one wants to learn more.

Fourth, the book will be available in english and in Malagasy.
As a corrollary, it will usher a standard about all those terms in Malagasy.
And remove language barrier.
We expect it to be useful not only for Malagasy but who struggles with quantitative skills.
